### Criteria for Assessing the Risk of an AI System

The assessment of the risk associated with an AI system is a crucial process, especially for those classified as high-risk. Here are the key criteria that are used in this evaluation:

1. **Intended Purpose**: The assessment considers the specific goals and contexts for which the AI system is designed to operate. This includes how the AI system is expected to function and the implications of its use.

2. **Data Processing**: Evaluators must examine the nature and amount of data that the AI system processes. This includes determining whether any special categories of personal data are involved, which could heighten risks related to privacy and discrimination.

3. **Autonomy and Human Oversight**: The level of autonomy the AI system possesses is critical. If the system can operate independently, the assessment includes evaluating the potential for human override of decisions made by the AI, particularly in scenarios that may lead to harm.

4. **Previous Incidents**: Historical performance and any documented incidents involving the AI system play a significant role in risk evaluation. Reports of previous harm or adverse impacts provide context for current assessments.

5. **Vulnerable Populations**: Special attention is given to how the AI system's outcomes may disproportionately affect vulnerable groups. Identifying potential power imbalances between users and deployers can inform the risk profile effectively.

6. **Correctability of Outcomes**: Assessing whether outcomes produced by the AI system are easily correctable or reversible is vital. Outcomes that adversely impact health, safety, or fundamental rights and are not easily rectifiable elevate the risk classification.

### Conclusion

These criteria integrate various dimensions of safety, ethical implications, performance evaluation, and regulatory compliance to ensure that AI systems are deployed responsibly while minimizing risks to users and society.

### Importance of Management

Understanding and managing systemic risks is vital for ensuring the stability and integrity of various societal frameworks affected by AI technologies. During the development and deployment processes, stakeholders must assess these risks to mitigate potential far-reaching consequences.
